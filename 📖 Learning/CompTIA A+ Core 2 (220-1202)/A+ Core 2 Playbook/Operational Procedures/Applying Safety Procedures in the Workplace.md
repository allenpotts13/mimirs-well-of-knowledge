# <span class="rune">ᚲ</span> Applying Safety Procedures in the Workplace

> *ESD protects the equipment. Electrical safety protects you. Never confuse which procedure serves which goal.*

---

## 🎯 Problem

A scenario describes a physical repair or workplace situation. Identify the correct safety procedure.

---

## 🤔 Mental Model

| Signal in the scenario | Answer |
|---|---|
| "handling a bare motherboard or expansion card" | ESD strap and mat |
| "storing or shipping a spare component" | Antistatic bag |
| "about to open a desktop case for repair" | Disconnect power first |
| "moving a heavy piece of equipment" | Proper lifting technique |
| "a device is smoking or sparking" | Fire safety procedure — power off, do not use water on electrical fires |
| "cleaning dust from inside a case" | Compressed air/vacuum designed for electronics, safety goggles |
| "cabling is creating a trip hazard or airflow blockage" | Cable management |
| "handling toner during printer maintenance" | Avoid inhalation, follow MSDS guidance, safety goggles/mask as needed |

---

## 🧠 Why This Pattern Works

Split every safety scenario into "does this protect the component" or "does this protect the person." ESD-specific gear (straps, mats, antistatic bags) exists purely to protect sensitive electronics from static discharge — it does nothing for human safety. Electrical/personal safety (disconnecting power, lifting technique, fire safety, goggles/masks) exists purely to protect the technician. A scenario rarely needs both answers at once, so identifying which category it's testing narrows the choices immediately.

---

## 🚨 Common Mistakes

- Reaching for an ESD strap when the real hazard in the scenario is electrical (should disconnect power) or physical (should use proper lifting technique)
- Assuming any dust-cleaning method is fine — improper compressed air use or the wrong tool can damage components or create new ESD risk
- Treating fire safety around electronics the same as any fire — some extinguishing methods are unsafe on live equipment

---

## 💡 Wisdom from Mímir

Ask "am I protecting the part or protecting myself?" first — it sorts every safety-procedure question into the right half of the list before you even look at the answer choices.
