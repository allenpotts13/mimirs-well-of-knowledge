# <span class="rune">ᚲ</span> Using Proper Communication Techniques and Professionalism

> *The "obviously polite" answer is the correct one almost every time — the exam is testing restraint, not cleverness.*

---

## 🎯 Problem

A scenario describes a customer interaction. Identify the professional, correct response.

---

## 🤔 Mental Model

| Signal in the scenario | Answer |
|---|---|
| "customer is angry or raising their voice" | Actively listen, don't argue or get defensive |
| "customer's description of the problem is vague" | Ask open-ended clarifying questions, restate to confirm understanding |
| "technician is running late to an appointment" | Contact the customer proactively |
| "technician's phone rings during a customer visit" | Avoid the distraction — don't take personal calls |
| "issue can't be fixed on this visit" | Set clear expectations and a timeline, communicate status |
| "customer's private files are visible during the repair" | Handle confidential material with discretion |
| "job is finished" | Document the service provided, follow up later to confirm satisfaction |
| "customer disputes the technician's assessment" | Avoid being dismissive or judgmental; clarify calmly |

---

## 🧠 Why This Pattern Works

Every professionalism question is testing whether you'll choose the response that de-escalates and respects the customer, versus the response that's technically efficient but socially wrong. The correct answer is consistently: listen first, don't argue, communicate clearly and proactively, protect confidentiality, and close the loop afterward. There's no clever exception to this pattern on the exam — professionalism questions reward the boring, correct choice every time.

---

## 🚨 Common Mistakes

- Picking an answer that prioritizes speed or technical correctness over the customer's experience
- Assuming "explain the technical details" is always right — matching jargon to the audience matters, and over-explaining can read as dismissive
- Treating a late arrival as a minor detail not worth a proactive heads-up call

---

## 💡 Wisdom from Mímir

If two answers both seem reasonable, pick the one that treats the customer with more patience and more communication — that's the test's actual bias.
