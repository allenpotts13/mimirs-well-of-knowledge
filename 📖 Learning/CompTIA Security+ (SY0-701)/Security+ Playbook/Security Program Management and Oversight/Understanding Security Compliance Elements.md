# <span class="rune">ᚲ</span> Understanding Security Compliance Elements

> *Fines are just one consequence of non-compliance — reputational damage and lost licenses can hurt more.*

---

## 🎯 Problem

A scenario describes a compliance or privacy situation. Identify the correct element or consequence.

---

## 🤔 Mental Model

| Signal in the scenario | Answer |
|---|---|
| "a company reports its compliance status to its own leadership" | Internal compliance reporting |
| "a regulator reviews the company's compliance directly" | External compliance reporting |
| "a company is formally penalized financially for a violation" | Fines |
| "a company loses its ability to legally operate in an industry" | Loss of license |
| "customers/partners lose trust after a public violation" | Reputational damage |
| "ongoing verification that controls are actually being followed" | Compliance monitoring (due diligence/due care) |
| "a customer requests their personal data be permanently erased" | Right to be forgotten |
| "a company determines what personal data it holds and how long it's kept" | Data inventory and retention |
| "an entity decides why/how personal data is processed" | Data controller |

---

## 🧠 Why This Pattern Works

Compliance elements split into **reporting** (communicating status, internal or external), **consequences** (what happens when compliance fails), and **monitoring** (ongoing verification, not a one-time check). Privacy overlays a data-subject-centric layer on top of general compliance — rights like "right to be forgotten" and roles like controller/processor matter specifically because personal data carries additional legal obligations beyond general regulatory compliance.

---

## 🚨 Common Mistakes

- Assuming fines are the only meaningful consequence of non-compliance — reputational damage and loss of license can be more damaging long-term
- Treating compliance reporting and compliance monitoring as the same activity — reporting is periodic communication, monitoring is continuous verification
- Confusing due diligence (research before a decision) with due care (ongoing responsible practice) in a compliance monitoring context
- Forgetting privacy rights (like right to be forgotten) apply specifically to personal/regulated data, not all company data generally

---

## 💡 Wisdom from Mímir

When a compliance scenario asks about consequences, resist naming only "fines" — list the full range (fines, sanctions, reputational damage, loss of license, contractual impacts) before picking the one that best matches the scenario's specifics.
